Pass 1: what the solver, and the model, do to the movie

Thirteen takes of one scene. Same prompt, same seed, same 1024×1024, same 124 frames, same checkpoint. No second pass anywhere. The first thirteen change only the pass-1 sampler, scheduler and step count on the base model; the last six swap the model itself for MiniMax's 8-step ACC/PDD LoRA and FastVideo's 4-step FastH3 VSA checkpoint. Which ones read best to you?

Why we ran it: we froze a fresh pass 1 for a pass-2 sampler study and the clips came back visibly unlike the scene we bench against. Not a variation on it, a different take. So we measured it.

The control that makes the rest readable

Re-running the exact recipe behind our shipped clip today, three weeks and a core update later, reproduces it at 39.9 dB, tighter than that clip's own re-encode at 37.8 dB. The pipeline is reproducible, so every difference below is the setting and not drift in the stack. Every other take lands 14-18 dB from the reference, further apart than two different pass-2 samplers on an identical pass 1 (23-26 dB). There seems to be no small change to pass 1: touch any part of the solve and you get a different movie rather than a nudged one.

Read the clips, not the table

drift is distance from the reference take, not quality. motion is mean frame-to-frame luminance difference, and on a lateral pan that is dominated by the camera, so it ranks gross energy and cannot tell you whether a character's turn reads well. That is the part we want eyes on. We have our own ordering and are deliberately not stating it so as not to anchor yours.

What it does not explain

Drift does not track preference: in our own viewing the two furthest-drifting takes sit at opposite ends of the ranking. Step count is not uniform either, and even changes sign: res_multistep + simple and er_sde + beta gain gross motion as steps rise, while both gradient_estimation recipes lose it. One scene, one seed, so treat all of it as a starting point.
